## Programme Overview

AI for Data Stewards is a practical four-day programme that connects organisational data, governance,  business operations and AI-enabled decision-making. It is designed for professionals who understand their  functions and data but are not expected to be AI engineers or data scientists. Participants with basic to  moderate AI familiarity learn how to identify worthwhile uses of AI, evaluate data readiness, verify AI outputs,  apply appropriate controls and translate opportunities into measurable business workflows.

The programme follows a clear capability journey: Understand, identify, apply, verify, govern, implement and  measure. Its emphasis is enterprise application and responsible use, not product-specific technical training.

The four-day programme also incorporates a professional development and accreditation pathway aligned  with the CMI Leading AI Implementation programme. This connects responsible AI knowledge with the  managerial capability required to lead adoption, manage change and demonstrate operational impact.

## DIEZ Digital AI Initiatives

The training covers all seven initiatives identified in the client guidelines. Participants consider the business  purpose, required data, likely risks, human role, controls and measures of success for each initiative.

| Initiative | Business application | Training coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer Segmentation | Groups customers by behaviour and profile to target engagement and services. | Data completeness and representation, segment validity, historical bias, business interpretation and appropriate use. |
| Sentiment Analysis | Reads customer feedback across channels to identify satisfaction trends. | Language and context, confidence, false interpretation, multilingual considerations, trend validation and manual review. |
| Customer Churn Prediction | Flags customers who may leave so teams can intervene. | Predictive data, probability rather than certainty, fairness, intervention rules, outcome tracking and human approval. |
| Content Moderation | Screens submitted content against compliance standards. | Policy translation, classification errors, confidence thresholds, sensitive content, exception handling and escalation. |
| Partner IQ | Matches businesses with suitable partners through AI-supported matchmaking. | Matching criteria, data quality, ranking bias, explainability, conflicts, human judgement and partnership outcomes. |
| Copilot | Supports daily work, drafting and information lookup. | Prompting, authorised data, confidentiality, source checking, output verification, accountability and appropriate task selection. |
| Document Intelligence | Extracts and structures document data to accelerate processing. | Extraction accuracy, missing information, confidence, validation, evidence retention, privacy and exception workflows. |


## Core Working Discipline

Participants develop a repeatable approach that remains useful as tools change:

CONTEXT  →  INSTRUCT  →  REVIEW  →  VERIFY  →  DECIDE  →  DOCUMENT  →  ACT OR ESCALATE

Exercises can be completed in DIEZ-approved AI environments using authorised, non-sensitive or simulated  information, including Microsoft Copilot where appropriate.

## Learning Outcomes

•  Identify activities where AI can improve efficiency, consistency, insight or decision support, while  recognising where direct human judgement is required.

•  Provide suitable context and instructions, work iteratively with AI and improve generated outputs.

•  Verify accuracy, identify unsupported statements and decide whether an output should be accepted,  improved, rejected or escalated.

•  Use AI appropriately for metadata drafting, data-quality investigation, classification, summarisation,  governance documentation and stakeholder communication.

•  Assess whether data is documented, governed, accessible and fit for an AI-enabled use case.

•  Recognise privacy, confidentiality, hallucination, bias, security, intellectual-property and over-reliance  risks.

•  Define human review, decision rights, audit evidence, escalation, monitoring and success measures in AI- assisted workflows.

•  Evaluate the purpose, data, risks, controls and success measures for all seven DIEZ digital AI initiatives.

•  Apply AI to DIEZ-relevant scenarios involving leasing, real estate pricing, government services, customer  growth, compliance and operations.

•  Demonstrate management capability aligned with CMI Leading AI Implementation through the capstone,  evidence portfolio and workplace application plan.

## Pre-Programme Baseline

A 30-45 minute self-paced diagnostic assesses AI familiarity and usage, confidence, understanding of AI  limitations, data-governance awareness, responsible-use awareness and confidence in evaluating AI- generated information. Each participant also identifies a recurring business, process or data activity that  could benefit from AI. The diagnostic is not a pass/fail test; it enables facilitators to calibrate the delivery to  the cohort.

## Applied Learning Method

Every day combines essential theory, discussion, demonstration, hands-on practice, challenge and evidence.  Participants produce usable artefacts throughout the programme rather than waiting for a final workshop. The  learning cycle is:

LEARN → SEE → TRY → CHALLENGE → IMPROVE → EVIDENCE

## Capstone and Responsible Workflow Design

On Day 4, participants develop a governed AI-assisted workflow covering the business problem, AI  opportunity, required data, data readiness, risks, human oversight, governance controls, implementation  approach, expected value and measurement. The operating model is:

INPUT  →  AI ASSISTANCE  →  HUMAN REVIEW  →  DECISION  →  EVIDENCE  →  ACTION  →  MONITORING

## CMI Accreditation and Professional Recognition

Wellington proposes an integrated accreditation and professional recognition pathway aligned with the CMI  Leading AI Implementation programme. The CMI component runs through the four-day learning journey rather  than operating as a separate course. The CMI-aligned content covers AI-enabled operational improvement, translating an AI opportunity into an  implementation plan, leading teams through change, responsible managerial decision-making, adoption,  team capability and the demonstration of operational impact.

Participants build evidence through practical activities, the governed AI workflow, the capstone assessment  and the individual 30-day application plan. Subject to final CMI programme mapping and approval, participant  registration, successful completion of the required assessment evidence and applicable eligibility

## Overall Programme Value

The programme equips the people at the centre of enterprise AI adoption to connect business need, data  readiness, responsible AI practice and operational implementation. Participants do not need to become AI  engineers. They need sufficient AI understanding to use it, data understanding to question it, governance  knowledge to control it and business judgement to implement it responsibly. The CMI pathway adds  recognised management development and provides a structured route to evidence their ability to lead  responsible AI implementation.

UNDERSTAND → APPLY → VERIFY → GOVERN → IMPLEMENT → MEASURE
