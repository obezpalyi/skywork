# AI for Data Stewards

Proposed four-day course plan

## Purpose

A practical, responsible-AI capability programme for practising data stewards and adjacent data-governance professionals. The course connects AI opportunity selection, data readiness, verification, governance and implementation.

Prepared for: Dubai Integrated Economic Zones Authority (DIEZ)

Prepared by: AI training capability provider (proposed delivery team)

Format: Four consecutive full days, with pre-course diagnostic and post-course assessment

Planning basis: DIEZ operating context, UAE federal requirements and Dubai government data, digital and information-security instruments

Version: Proposed plan | September 2026

## DIEZ | AI for Data Stewards

## 1. Executive summary

This plan proposes a four-day applied course for DIEZ data stewards, business data owners, operational managers and business use-case owners. It is designed for professionals who understand their functions and data but are not expected to become AI engineers or data scientists. The course builds a repeatable discipline for deciding where AI adds value, testing whether data is fit for purpose, verifying AI outputs, applying proportionate controls and translating opportunities into measurable workflows.

The plan is designed for DIEZ’s operating context: an economic-zone ecosystem supporting businesses, innovation, digital services and technology-enabled growth. It turns that context into a practical capability journey—understand, identify, apply, verify, govern, implement and measure—with timed sessions, participant evidence, facilitation methods and decision points. DIEZ’s public positioning emphasises business enablement, digital transformation and advanced technologies, including AI-enabled operational improvement [7].

## Design principle

The course is technology-aware but not tool-led. Participants practise with approved or simulated information in DIEZ-approved environments, then apply professional judgement to accept, improve, reject or escalate an AI output. UAE and Dubai legal, policy and information-security controls are treated as part of the workflow—not as a separate compliance lecture.

## 2. UAE and Dubai alignment

This course is an operational training design, not legal advice. Before delivery, DIEZ’s legal, data-protection, information-security and data-governance owners should confirm the exact instruments and internal policies that apply to each scenario, dataset and technology environment.

| Local requirement / context | How the course operationalises it |
| --- | --- |
| UAE Personal Data Protection Law — Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 [1] | Teach purpose limitation, data minimisation, access control, retention, processor/vendor review, incident escalation and cross-border transfer checks where personal data is in scope. The law expressly excludes government data and governmental entities that control or process personal data; the course therefore treats it as a baseline for provider/contractor and other in-scope processing, while pairing it with Dubai government instruments. |
| Dubai Law No. 26 of 2015 Regulating Data Dissemination and Exchange [2] | Use data inventory, classification, provenance, release/approval, authorised sharing and evidence of permitted use as mandatory steps in every data-readiness and capstone exercise. |
| Dubai Data and Statistics Establishment / Dubai Data framework [2] | Practise documenting data owners, custodians, quality issues, metadata, reuse conditions and escalation routes for datasets used in government or cross-entity workflows. |
| Dubai Law No. 9 of 2022 Regulating the Provision of Digital Services [2] | Frame AI use around service quality, user impact, accessibility, continuity, human escalation and accountable change control for digital government services. |
| Dubai ICT Policies — Executive Council Resolution No. 15 of 2022 [2] | Require participants to identify the approved platform, identity/access path, hosting and integration constraints, records/evidence needs and responsible owner before proposing an AI-enabled workflow. |
| Dubai Information Security Regulation — Executive Council Resolution No. 13 of 2012 [2] | Embed least privilege, secure handling, no unapproved public upload, logging/evidence, incident reporting and controlled testing into labs and capstones. |
| Dubai's digital-government and economic-zone context [7] | Use Dubai-relevant cases: government services, leasing, real-estate pricing, customer growth, compliance, operational processing, |

## 3. Proposed course at a glance

| Element | Proposed design |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | Practising data stewards and adjacent data-governance, data-quality, business-data and operational professionals. |
| Duration | Four consecutive full days; alternative cadence is two two-day blocks within two weeks. |
| Pre-work | 30-45 minute self-paced diagnostic and one recurring stewardship task or use case. |
| Daily rhythm | Learn → see → try → challenge → improve → evidence. |

## 4. Intended outcomes

By the end of the course, participants should be able to:

 Identify stewardship and operational activities where AI can improve efficiency, consistency, insight or decision support—and recognise where direct human judgement is required.

 Frame a task with sufficient context and instructions, work iteratively with AI, and improve generated outputs.

 Verify accuracy, identify unsupported statements and decide whether an output should be accepted, revised, rejected or escalated.

 Use AI appropriately for metadata drafting, data-quality investigation, classification, summarisation, governance documentation and stakeholder communication.

 Assess whether data is documented, governed, accessible and fit for an AI-enabled use case.

 Recognise privacy, confidentiality, hallucination, bias, security, intellectual-property and over-reliance risks.

 Define human review, decision rights, audit evidence, escalation, monitoring and success measures in AI- assisted workflows.

 Evaluate purpose, data, risks, controls and success measures for all seven DIEZ digital AI initiatives.

 Translate one opportunity into a governed workflow and a practical 30-day implementation plan.

## 5. DIEZ initiative coverage

The seven initiatives below are used as recurring case studies, exercises and capstone options. Participants examine each initiative through the same lenses: business purpose, required data, readiness, likely risks, human role, controls and measures of success.

| DIEZ initiative | Stewardship / responsible-AI lens | Example evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer Segmentation | Completeness, representation, segment validity, historical bias and business interpretation. | Segment review and data-readiness notes. |
| Sentiment Analysis | Language and context, confidence, false interpretation, multilingual considerations and trend validation. | Reviewed sentiment summary and validation log. |
| Customer Churn Prediction | Probability rather than certainty, fairness, intervention rules, outcomes and human approval. | Use-case canvas and intervention control. |
| Content Moderation | Policy translation, classification errors, confidence thresholds, sensitive content, exception handling and escalation. | Safe-use checklist and escalation map. |
| Partner IQ | Matching criteria, ranking bias, explainability, conflicts, human judgement and partnership outcomes. | Matching-criteria review and decision-rights map. |
| Copilot | Prompting, authorised data, confidentiality, source checking, output verification and accountability. | Tested prompt pattern and verification record. |
| Document Intelligence | Extraction accuracy, missing information, confidence, evidence retention, privacy and exception workflows. | Extraction QA record and exception workflow. |

## 7. Capstone design

Each participant or small group develops one governed AI-assisted workflow from a DIEZ-relevant scenario. Capstones should be operationally credible without requiring production data or a production deployment.

| Capstone component | Required question | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business problem | What outcome or service issue is being improved? | Problem statement and affected users. |
| AI opportunity | What will AI assist with, and what remains human-owned? | Task boundary and human-accountability statement. |
| Data readiness | Is the data documented, governed, accessible and fit for purpose? | Readiness checklist and gaps. |
| Risk and controls | What could go wrong, and what controls reduce the risk? | Risk review, safe-use decisions and evidence rules. |
| Human oversight | Who reviews, decides, approves exceptions and escalates? | Decision-rights and escalation map. |
| Workflow | How does the work run end to end? | Input → assistance → review → decision → evidence → action → monitoring. |
| Implementation | What is needed to adopt this safely? | Dependencies, roles, change actions and 30-day experiment. |
| Measures | How will value, quality, safety and adoption be monitored? | Leading and lagging indicators. |


## Recommended capstone scenarios

Leasing operations; real-estate pricing support; government-service request handling; customer growth or retention; compliance triage; operational document processing. Final scenario pack to be confirmed with DIEZ.

## 8. Assessment and evidence model

Assessment is developmental and evidence-based. The pre-assessment is not a pass/fail gate; it calibrates the cohort and establishes a baseline.

| Stage | Method | Evidence / purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before | 30-45 minute diagnostic, confidence survey and nominated recurring task. | Baseline of AI familiarity, limitations awareness, data-governance awareness and candidate opportunity. |
| During | Facilitator observation, peer review, pulse checks, daily artefact checks and scenario challenges. | Checks conceptual understanding and practical stewardship decisions as they develop. |
| Final | Scenario-based post-assessment, capstone review and 30-day transfer plan. | Measures progress from baseline and confirms the participant can design a responsible AI-assisted workflow. |
| Optional follow-up | Manager check-in or community-of-practice session after 30 days. | Captures implementation progress, blockers and evidence of adoption. |

## 9. Learning resources and participant toolkit

The following resources are recommended for each participant. They are designed to support both the course and workplace transfer.

 Participant workbook with plain-language AI concepts, guided exercises, reflection prompts and role-specific application space.

 AI practice toolkit: prompt canvas, output-verification checklist, safe-use decision guide, risk-review template and human-oversight template.

 Stewardship scenario pack: simulated datasets, metadata records, issue logs, governance requests and DIEZ- relevant cases.

 Transfer pack: job aids, glossary, capstone template, CMI evidence guide and 30-day application plan.

 Facilitator pack: session plans, worked examples, challenge cards, observation checklist, assessment materials and marking guidance.

 Whether delivery is four consecutive days or two two-day blocks within two weeks.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation in this plan |
| --- | --- |
| Participants use sensitive data in an unapproved tool. | Use safe, authorised or simulated information; define tool and data rules during mobilisation; include a safe-use decision guide. |
| Course becomes tool training rather than capability building. | Use a tool-neutral discipline and scenario evidence; treat tools as examples inside a broader workflow. |
| Outputs are accepted without verification. | Make review and evidence capture mandatory in every lab; assess accept/revise/reject/escalate decisions. |
| Scenarios are not relevant to DIEZ operations. | Validate cases with DIEZ SMEs and use the seven initiatives plus leasing, pricing, government-service, growth, compliance and operations contexts. |
| Learning does not transfer to workplace practice. | Finish with a governed workflow, a 30-day application plan and an optional manager/community follow-up. |
| Bilingual support fragments the course experience. | Use one shared curriculum and assessment standard with Arabic-speaking support integrated into facilitation. |

## 14. Recommended decision

Proceed with the four-day course design as the baseline for client discussion, subject to a short mobilisation workshop with DIEZ. Once confirmed, the timetable can be converted into a facilitator guide, participant workbook, scenario pack and assessment pack without changing the core learning journey.
