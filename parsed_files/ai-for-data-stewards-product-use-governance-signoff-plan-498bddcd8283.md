## DIEZ

# AI for Data Stewards

Product use, governance and sign-off programme

## Purpose

A four-day working programme for DIEZ managers and data stewards who will use, oversee or sign off AI-enabled products. Participants work with simulated DIEZ-style materials, practise in the approved Microsoft 365 Copilot environment and leave with evidence-backed controls they can apply at work.

Prepared for: Dubai Integrated Economic Zones Authority (DIEZ)

Format: Four consecutive full days; net session times are confirmed with DIEZ HR

Working environment: Microsoft 365 Copilot on DIEZ laptops; simulated data only; no live-system access

Version: Delivery-authored draft | September 2026

## DIEZ | AI for Data Stewards

## 1. Executive summary

This is a practical four-day programme for DIEZ business section heads, team managers, data owners and stewards who will use, oversee or sign off AI-enabled products. Its centre of gravity is not a tour of AI concepts. It is the work participants will need to do when an AI product produces a tier, label, alert, ranking, draft or extracted field and someone must decide what happens next.

The seven DIEZ initiatives provide the working material: Customer Segmentation, Sentiment Analysis, Customer Churn Prediction, Content Moderation, Partner IQ, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Document Intelligence. Participants work in functional teams across DAFZ, DSO and Dubai CommerCity, using simulated DIEZ-style data. They leave with a traceable evidence portfolio, a product sign-off checklist and a responsible-use workflow they can take back to their function.

## How the programme is designed

The approved assistant is Microsoft 365 Copilot. It is the environment used in the labs, not the subject of the training. The recurring discipline is: CONTEXT →INSTRUCT →REVIEW →VERIFY →DECIDE →DOCUMENT →ACT OR ESCALATE.

## 2. Design decisions made for this cohort

| Decision | Why it is in the programme |
| --- | --- |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot is the only lab assistant. | It is the approved DIEZ environment confirmed for use in the labs. Participants learn safe practice in one controlled environment rather than comparing vendors. |
| All lab data is simulated. | No real customer data or financial values are used, and participants do not access live DIEZ systems. This keeps the exercises safe while preserving DIEZ-style decisions and data defects. |
| Teams are formed across zones by function. | Putting comparable roles from DAFZ, DSO and Dubai CommerCity together makes differences in definitions, data quality and process visible. |
| Day 3 includes a product sign-off simulation. | Participants need to practise the moment Digital asks a business owner or steward to accept an AI-enabled product, including testing evidence, limitations, human review and monitoring. |
| Unconfirmed product details stay in scope. | Content Moderation technology, Partner IQ mechanics and some owners are awaiting DIEZ knowledge. The sessions teach the responsible decisions around those products without inventing how they work. |
| DIEZ frameworks replace generic policy language when available. | The programme uses placeholders until DIEZ shares its data- and AI-governance extracts. Faculty do not write DIEZ policy during the course. |


## 3. UAE and Dubai alignment

This course is an operational training design, not legal advice. UAE and Dubai instruments are used as proposed guardrails only where DIEZ confirms they apply. DIEZ’s internal governance wording, data classifications, acceptable-use rules and escalation routes take precedence when supplied.

## 5. Intended outcomes

By the end of the course, participants should be able to:

 Explain what each of the seven DIEZ AI products does, where its output appears and which business decision or workflow it supports.

 Interpret customer tiers, sentiment labels, confidence scores, churn alerts, moderation classifications, partner rankings, Copilot responses and extracted document fields without treating them as certainty.

 Identify the data definitions, quality conditions and ownership required for each product to operate responsibly.

 Use Microsoft 365 Copilot responsibly in PowerPoint, Excel, Word and Outlook with approved information and appropriate verification.

 Verify product outputs and decide whether to accept, investigate, override, correct or escalate them.

 Recognise privacy, confidentiality, hallucination, bias, security, intellectual-property and over-reliance risks.

 Apply product-specific human review, decision rights, evidence retention, exception handling and monitoring.

 Decide whether a use case is safe to adopt, should be adopted only with a human in the loop, or is not ready to be solved by AI.

 Know what evidence is required before signing off an AI product and how to escalate a concern.

 Create a governed AI-assisted workflow and a practical 30-day application plan.

## 8. Capstone design

Each team carries one prioritised DIEZ use case into the capstone. It may be an improvement to an existing initiative or a new use case from the team’s own pain points. The result is a governed workflow, not a production build. Teams use their Day 1–3 artefacts and simulated material.

| Capstone component | Required question | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and value | What user or process problem is being improved, and why is AI appropriate? | Problem statement, affected users and value hypothesis. |
| Product and purpose | Which DIEZ product or new use case is in scope and what approved business purpose does it serve? | Product/workflow, users and purpose statement. |
| Output and decision | What output does the product produce, and what remains human-owned? | Output interpretation and human-accountability statement. |
| Data readiness | Is the data documented, governed, accessible and fit for purpose? | Readiness checklist and gaps. |
| Risk and controls | What could go wrong, and what controls reduce the risk? | Risk review, safe-use decisions and evidence rules. |
| Human oversight | Who reviews, decides, approves exceptions and escalates? | Decision-rights and escalation map. |
| Workflow | How does the work run end to end? | Input → assistance → review → decision → evidence → action → monitoring. |
| Implementation | What is needed to adopt this safely? | Dependencies, roles, change actions and 30-day experiment. |
| Measures | How will value, quality, safety and adoption be monitored? | Leading and lagging indicators. |

## Capstone choice

Teams may improve an existing DIEZ initiative or frame a new use case from a real pain point. In both cases, risk is weighed alongside value and every AI step has a named reviewer and evidence rule.

## 9. Assessment and evidence model

Assessment is developmental and evidence-based. The baseline is not a pass/fail gate; it calibrates the cohort and provides a before-and-after comparison.

| Stage | Method | Evidence / purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before | 30-45 minute diagnostic and confidence survey across the seven products. | Baseline of product familiarity, current use, limitations awareness, data-governance awareness and role responsibility. Include at least one scenario item per outcome O1-O6. |
| During | Facilitator observation, peer review, pulse checks, daily artefact checks and scenario challenges. | Checks conceptual understanding and practical stewardship decisions as they develop. |
| Final | Scenario-based post-assessment, capstone review and 30-day transfer plan. | Measures progress from baseline and confirms applied understanding. Include at least one scenario item per outcome O1-O6. |
| Optional follow-up | Manager check-in or community-of-practice session after 30 days. | Captures implementation progress, blockers and evidence of adoption. |


Suggested capstone rubric (to confirm with DIEZ)

| Criterion | Weight | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem framing and value | 20% | Specific user or process problem; credible benefit; appropriate AI boundary. |
| Data readiness | 20% | Data documentation, access, quality and representation are assessed with gaps stated. |
| Responsible controls | 25% | Risks, privacy/security, human review, decision rights and escalation are explicit. |
| Workflow and implementation | 20% | The workflow is repeatable, feasible and supported by roles, dependencies and adoption actions. |
| Measures and evidence | 15% | Success measures cover value, quality, safety, adoption and audit evidence. |

**14. Risks and mitigations**

| Risk | Mitigation in this plan |
| --- | --- |
| Participants use sensitive data in an unapproved tool. | Use safe, authorised or simulated information; define tool and data rules during mobilisation; include a safe-use decision guide. |
| Course becomes tool training rather than capability building. | Use Microsoft 365 Copilot as the controlled practice environment, but assess judgement, verification, sign-off and evidence rather than button knowledge. |
| Outputs are accepted without verification. | Make review and evidence capture mandatory in every lab; assess accept/revise/reject/escalate decisions. |
| Product details are assumed or outdated. | Validate the product fact sheets with named owners before delivery; keep unknown owners and unconfirmed technologies clearly labelled while still teaching the responsible decisions around those products. |
| Learners treat a product output as a final decision. | Assess interpretation of tiers, confidence scores, alerts, rankings and extracted fields; require human decision rules and evidence. |
| Scenarios are not relevant to DIEZ operations. | Build labs around the actual product workflows: Salesforce tiers, customer-care touchpoints, churn alerts, community moderation, zone matching, M365 Copilot and visa-document extraction. |
| Learning does not transfer to workplace practice. | Finish with a governed workflow, a 30-day application plan and an optional manager/community follow-up. |
| Bilingual support fragments the course experience. | Use one shared curriculum and assessment standard with Arabic-speaking support integrated into facilitation. |


## 15. Recommended decision

Proceed with this delivery-authored structure as the baseline for client review. Before the first delivery, DIEZ should provide the governance-framework extracts, confirm product owners and pending product details, validate the simulated materials, and approve the sign-off and escalation language. The programme can then be delivered without changing the four-day sequence; later cohorts can replace the placeholders with DIEZ’s own wording and current product information.
